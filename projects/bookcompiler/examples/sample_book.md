# Policy Shocks

This sample book is original test material for BookCompiler. It is not a real published book.

## Chapter 1: Incentives and Prices

A policy shock changes relative prices. When the cost of capital rises, firms delay investment. If the central bank tightens liquidity, banks reprice credit and households reduce spending. A useful analysis asks who bears the cost, which constraints bind, and what second-order effects follow.

## Chapter 2: Coalitions and Implementation

A regulation can be economically efficient but politically fragile. When veto players oppose the rule, implementation stalls. The government must build a coalition, compensate losers, and sequence the reform. Political feasibility is not the same as economic desirability.

## Chapter 3: Legal Authority and Risk

A regulator must act within statutory authority. If the rule exceeds jurisdiction, courts may stay it. Unless the law clearly delegates authority, legal risk is high. Legal analysis should separate authority, procedure, rights, liability, and enforceability.
